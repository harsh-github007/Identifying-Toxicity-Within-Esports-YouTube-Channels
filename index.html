READ THE RANGE

Zero is an estimate, not proof that a community is free of toxicity. Only comments still visible on YouTube were available to the study.

Why are the sample sizes different?

The pipeline sampled the latest 10 uploads per channel. Comment availability differs by channel and video. Gyan Gaming contributed 339 comments; the other channels contributed roughly 5,000 each. Unequal samples and sparse toxic labels limit comparisons.

CONTEXT MATTERS

Low precision in this pilot

Detoxify multilingual had just 1.3% precision in this sample. Praise, slang, creative spelling and romanised Hindi make generic toxicity detection unreliable here. The word list still missed more than half of toxic comments.

04 / THE RESEARCH

Transparent by design.

60 human labels. Five toxic examples.
The limits belong beside the findings.

Re-run in Colab ↗
  1. 01

    Collect visible comments

    YouTube’s API collects comments and replies from recent uploads. Author IDs are hashed, and overlap checks prevent duplicated channels.

  2. 02

    Keep human judgement in the loop

    A stratified sample over-represents flagged comments. Weights account for this sampling design. One annotator labelled 60 of the 400 drawn comments.

  3. 03

    Measure errors before estimating prevalence

    Compare word-list, Detoxify and character n-gram methods against labels. Correct all-comment estimates for measured sensitivity and specificity, with bootstrap uncertainty.

What this study cannot conclude

It cannot rank channels, see removed comments, or describe every video or future upload. One annotator and five toxic labels leave substantial uncertainty. Latin script does not establish language; sarcasm and context remain difficult.
